### Introduction

Finding a job is tough these days, as there is so much competition. Too many applicants, with very few jobs.

What can you do to make sure you are the only candidate that gets the job?

The answer is you can use the Premium LinkedIn subscription. After all isn’t that why you have a LinkedIn profile? To find a job or to be found for your next job?

Which subscription should I buy?

Did you know there are four paid subscriptions?

This eBook will help you make the right decision based on your own job search circumstances. By the end of the eBook you should be able to understand whether you should pay for it and how you can use the Premium Career subscription.

This eBook is written by a LinkedIn Author who not only teaches the subject, but also uses what he teaches. In 2017 I used LinkedIn Premium Career subscription to find a job.

Read on to find out more.

Thank you for choosing to read this book.

### LinkedIn Premium Career

The majority of this LinkedIn article will focus on the value of enrolling in the LinkedIn Premium Career subscription.

Below you will see what LinkedIn currently advertises that you get with a LinkedIn Premium Career subscription.

![](./images/00006.jpeg)

When you subscribe to LinkedIn Premium Career, you will receive a welcome message and also be added to a hidden [LinkedIn Premium Career group](#undefined). 

Judging by the group membership (804,254 as of today), less than 1% of the total member landscape on LinkedIn have purchased the LinkedIn Premier Career subscription.

**Cost**

The cost of the entry level Premium Career subscription at the time of this writing was $29.99 + sales tax. This is the monthly cost. If you pay up front, you pay ahead for 12 months at a price of $299.88 + sales tax. This is about a 25% savings. 

![](./images/00007.jpeg)

Note: Unlimited browsing is not part of the Premium Career subscription, unlike the Premium Business and Premium Executive. Even so, my LinkedIn profile browsing did not seem to be limited.

I am not going to go into the value of subscribing to the Premium Business and Premium Executive LinkedIn subscriptions in this article. 

Twelve months of LinkedIn Premium Career paid monthly would be $359.88 + sales tax. My recommendation is you pay monthly and forgo the discount. Here is why, for three reasons… 

1) The average job-seeker will take 6-10 months to find a new position if they are looking every day.

2) Do you really want to pay for something you don’t use after you have found a job? See my conclusion section. 

3) If you cancel your subscription after six months when you have paid the full 12 month subscription, you will lose that money, as there are no refunds given for early cancellation.

**Direct Messaging to Recruiters**

The value of Inmail messaging isn’t realized because most great recruiters on LinkedIn will either reach out to the candidate they are looking for, or you can reach out to them as you would normally do to grow your network on a daily/weekly basis. 

You get five Inmails with the lowest priced subscription, LinkedIn Premium Career. From the job seeker point of view, if you want to reach out to a recruiter, you can easily do that by connecting with them. 

After you have connected with them via LinkedIn, I recommend you telephone them; otherwise, your email could be lost in their inbox. 

Value score of this feature = zero.

**LinkedIn Tip:** You can get the name of the recruiter from the job page, then search their name and company name to connect with them, rather than use up an Inmail.

**Featured Applicant**

From testing this out, I didn’t have any success with any calls from any recruiters for jobs that I applied for. Perhaps that might just be me, but based on the zero responses, I am going to give a zero for this feature as well. 

Perhaps this feature doesn’t get enabled if the company hasn’t enabled the “Easy Apply” api.

Value score of this feature = zero.

**Online Video Courses**

This would perhaps be of value to college graduates. For seasoned career professionals, I doubt this feature would be of much use. 

If you start reviewing the training, you are going to be taking away time you could be using for your job search. 

Still, there are 9000+ courses available, according to a LinkedIn blog via @Gyanda Sachdeva, which you can review in detail here: <https://blog.linkedin.com/2017/february/9/whats-new-with-linkedin-premium> 

Since I did not use this service, I am not going review and rate the feature in this article. 

Here is what the LinkedIn site says, by a job posting: “Learn from expert video courses on LinkedIn Learning. Included with Premium.” 

Below is a sample. There are thousands of courses and you may search using keywords to find the right course for your situation.

**Who’s Viewed Your Profile**

In the basic FREE subscription you can see up to five profile views at any one time. 

With the LinkedIn Premium Career subscription, you can see all your profile views for the last 90 days. This feature is of value, as you can see who is looking at your profile and assess how long it might take you to find a job. 

Plus, you can make connections with members who might post a network update that advertises a position that needs to be filled quickly.

If you are only getting three to six profile views per 30 days, it could take you up to 10 months to find a new job.

In addition to the person viewing your account, you can find out what industry, company, and location the viewer is from. 

With the rollout of the new user interface in early 2017, much of the profile views dashboard has been removed. You can see some of those older features in Cherry’s blog. 

See @Cherry Kwan’s article — **LinkedIn Premium: Worth the Cost?** <https://www.semrush.com/blog/linkedin-premium-worth-the-cost/> 

**I believe Who has viewed my profile has to be one of the best features of the paid Premium Career subscription.**

Value score of this feature = 1.

**Applicant Insights**

Additionally, this is the part of the LinkedIn Premium Career subscription, along with the salary insights, that is worth paying for. What you get here is an assessment of how your profile stacks up against other applicants and a number of additional results as well, such as the following:

• Jobs where you are a top applicant

o Here you will see a tiled version of four company profiles where you have the best LinkedIn profile, as compared to other potential applicants.

• Competitive intelligence about other applicants

o Here you will see the section broken down further into categories, such as: Skills, Past Experience, Current Experience, Top Skills, Seniority Level, and Education.

• An inside look at the company and its employees

o Total employees

o Company-wide growth

o Average tenure

• Company talent sources

o Where did the employees get their degree from? The top listed degree from your university will be expressed as a percentage.

o This will be further broken down into schools and companies. Where did the latest recruits get hired from and what schools were they from, etc.?

• Meet the team

o Here you will see a list of people you are already connected to or should connect to if you are not already connected.

• Company page

o You will see the company page and a list of current followers.

o The company page will show the “About us” page from their LinkedIn company page.

• Similar jobs

o This is where you will get a list of jobs similar to the job you clicked on. This is very useful.

o There are typically nine cells of company jobs listed here. You can scroll further down and click on **See more jobs like this**.

• People also viewed

o Here you get inside knowledge of what other jobs are being clicked on. This is almost the same as the **Similar jobs** section of the job page.

• Salary insights

o This is one of the most interesting areas of the job page. You will get salary information based on information LinkedIn has gotten from surveys and job applications.

o As you can see below, the information will tell you two figures: Base salary and total compensation, including any other financial measured benefits. Keep in mind that these are estimates.

![](./images/00009.jpeg)

o See similar salary insights. In this sub-section, you will find additional information, which I have listed below. This will be available only if the information was provided to LinkedIn first via companies and job applicants.

▪ Similar titles

▪ Similar regions for this role

▪ Median base salary

▪ Median total compensation

▪ Additional compensation

▪ Top paying locations

▪ Open jobs for the role you clicked on originally

### Jobs on LinkedIn

Getting to see jobs on LinkedIn.com can be found via this link here: <https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/> 

I suspect that if you are in another country, like the UK, you can change the suffix to linkedin.co.uk/jobs, and that will work in the country where your membership is registered.

You jobs dashboard is divided into:

Metrics — Saved Jobs and Applied Jobs

Jobs where you are a top applicant

Jobs you might be interested in

LinkedIn Profinder — An advertisement for career coaches

Fastest growing companies for \[insert your profession\] professionals

Companies in your network — The companies you follow

When you click on a job you are interested in, you will get further information and here is where you find out if the value (your time) is considered. If you see a button that says **Apply on company website,** you will have to spend another 15-30 minutes filling in your information on the company’s jobsite.

This is where LinkedIn has a gap, because you should be able to use your LinkedIn profile to complete and apply for the job. However, very few companies have signed up to use LinkedIn’s job applicant api. 

I believe this is what makes LinkedIn the same as any other job listing site. You can’t use your LinkedIn personal details to apply for the job. Actually, in a conversation with a LinkedIn developer, their goal is to have every job opening in the world listed on LinkedIn.com. 

That’s a great goal to have, but how about changing it to a goal that every job listed must have the _Easy Apply_ option?

If the company with the job vacancy is using the LinkedIn job api, you should see the LinkedIn logo with “**Easy Apply.**”

In this case, you will see a dialogue box that comes up, similar to the screenshot below. All you need to do is confirm your phone number, email address, upload your current resume, and click the button, **Move application to the top of the recruiters list**. 

Then hit the **Submit application** button. Based on my research, only one in five companies I reviewed used the LinkedIn Easy Apply api.

### Should I buy LinkedIn Premium?

After using LinkedIn Premium Career for several months, I recommend you give it a try, at least for the first month. 

Look to see if you have the opportunity to get a month’s complimentary subscription. Also, I recommend you don’t enroll in the annual subscription, but instead request the month-to-month, in case you cancel and find a job opportunity early.

What I want you all to remember is that LinkedIn Premium Career is an online tool that can help you find and research jobs, but it won’t get you a job. Finding a job will be down to you and many other factors that are not listed here. 

Getting a job is a full-time job. Don’t invest the money if you are not going to invest the 20-40 hours a week that it takes to find a new position.

Check out my next LinkedIn article, _How to find a new job in 14 days or less_.

**Conclusion**

One improvement LinkedIn should make to this subscription, in order for me to see the return on investment, is to require more companies to sign up for the “Easy Apply” feature. 

Without that, the LinkedIn Premium Career subscription appears to be worth less.

Get LinkedIn Premium Career, but only if you are serious about getting a new role and understand all of the benefits you get with the lowest cost LinkedIn Premium subscription.

Again, I recommend you sign up for the complimentary month’s trial if it is available to you and make sure you choose the monthly subscription from the outset. 

Don’t worry about forgetting to cancel. LinkedIn will send you a reminder email a week before the free trial ends, so you have plenty of time to cancel.

LinkedIn has made it really easy to cancel via the **My subscription** setting under your mini-profile photo on the right hand side of the user interface. 

The reminder email also has a link to the **Cancel subscription** setting on LinkedIn.com.

The Premium Career subscription benefits include:

• LinkedIn Premium Badge

• Additional profile views, up to 90 days’ worth

• Five free InMails\*\*

• Increased search, no commercial search limit, but it is still limited and I’m not sure by how much\*

• Salary insights

• Cost of subscription (monthly)

• Additional job and company insights

• Skills insights

**One final item about skills: You can leverage LinkedIn Insights to ensure you have the right skills listed on your profile now and tomorrow. Here is how.**

**Steps to Leverage Skills Insights**

1\. Click on your Jobs dashboard at [https://www.linkedin.com/jobs ](#undefined)

[2](#undefined). Go to a job you are interested in, either in the jobs where you are a top applicant or in jobs you may be interested in.

3\. Once you have clicked on the job you are interested in, you will see a section that says, “Competitive intelligence about other applicants.” Focus here on the **Top skills** section. It will say something like, “You have six out of the 10 top skills,” or whatever number applies to your LinkedIn profile. Make a note of the skills you do not have. I recommend you copy and paste them to a screen capture program of choice.

4\. Open up another tab in your browser and type in LinkedIn.com.

5\. Edit your profile by clicking on your picture on the lefthand side.

6\. Do a CTRL+F and search for the skills section of your profile. You are going to add one of the skills you do not have on your profile. In this example I will use Team Leadership.

7\. Click on the **Add a new skill** pencil icon.

8\. If you have 50 skills already added to your profile, remove one by deleting the skill that has the least amount of endorsements and may not be relevant to the role you are seeking. You will have to save the change before you can add a new skill. Then go back and edit the skill, as in step 7\. Right under the **Add skill** title, type in the skill you want to add. In this case, it’s “Team Leadership,” without the quotes.

9\. Click save.

10\. Now go back to step 7 and you will see that the skill is at the bottom of the list. If you want to move it to the top, you can do that by clicking on the three lines icon and dragging it to the top. I recommend you make this new skill the fourth skill listed on your profile. Since it is a new skill, you will have zero endorsements and it’s better to have the top three endorsed skills that are important to you listed first by number of endorsements.

Repeat steps six to 10 for any more skills you want to add to your profile to match the job you are interested in.

Now you will be a 100% match for the skills needed for the position. You can do this for current jobs and any new roles for the future. Your skills section should now look something like I have shown below.

![](./images/00011.jpeg)

**When you are able to single out the best job for you, LinkedIn will tell you what skills this role is looking for. You can leverage this information for your LinkedIn profile, then update your profile accordingly.**

**Note1: If you find a job that does not have any applicants, it will be likely that you cannot follow these steps, as there are no analytics provided. LinkedIn needs at least 10 applicants to apply before this information is shared with your potential application.**

**Note2: This tip works even if you do not have the LinkedIn Premium Career subscription.**

**\*I was unable to test the limit of the search feature.**

**\*\*Some of these benefits may be different in your country of residence.**
