[]{#06_Foreword.xhtml#page_9}**Foreword**

I had my first contact with social networks in 2004: I had created an
account at OpenBC. Today, this network is called Xing. This was followed
by Facebook in 2007 and Twitter in 2009. Then, in 2012, Instagram was
added and, shortly after that, the business network LinkedIn.

My reach on LinkedIn grew very quickly because I was able to present my
passion for digital marketing in the best possible way here. A topic
that seems rather boring on Facebook and is almost impossible to present
on Instagram. As a digital marketer, I can also use many of LinkedIn\'s
options and I try to optimize my LinkedIn presence on a daily basis.

The decisive factor for the decision to write a book about
self-marketing and the possibilities of personal growth on LinkedIn was
then the following fact:

Through my regular posts on LinkedIn, I generated a certain amount of
attention, so colleagues and friends gradually contacted me to ask if I
could give them some tips. Some asked for specific training. Gradually,
I was also asked to give talks on \"LinkedIn Growth Hacking\" and booked
for corporate training on \"Optimal Presentation on LinkedIn\". I was
asked if my presentations were recorded and available online. Whether I
could possibly share the material so that it could be read. I was also
approached by contacts of mine asking if I could do something to help
them build their own personal brand on LinkedIn. But also,
completely[]{#06_Foreword.xhtml#page_10} unknown people who felt
inspired by my activities on LinkedIn contacted me.

Since I myself work in digital marketing for a company and my daily work
keeps me busy, it was difficult for me to handle the number of requests
in parallel. Therefore, it initially occurred to me to make my lectures
available as videos. But then I had the idea to write down the trainings
I gave in detail and the idea for this book was born.

I hope that I can give every interested networker helpful tips for their
own presentation on LinkedIn. In my book, I would like to focus in
particular on the possibility of growing one\'s own network through
small measures, so-called hacks.

I am convinced that self-marketing will become increasingly important in
professional life in the future. Social media offer a good opportunity
to present oneself in the best possible way and to occupy certain terms.

Finally, I would like to note that the statements in this book represent
my own opinion and my own experience. Of course, I have tried to support
the statements with literature, studies or interviews. For this purpose,
both German- and English-language sources were consulted.

My opinion does not necessarily reflect the opinion of LinkedIn or the
attitude of my employer. I received no compensation from my employer,
LinkedIn or any other company mentioned in this book for writing this
book or for merely mentioning it.

[]{#06_Foreword.xhtml#page_11}I also made a conscious decision to
publish the book in self-publishing format. Even though I have contact
with well-known publishers who would certainly have guaranteed me a much
better distribution performance, what counts for me is speed. A book on
LinkedIn has to be on the market quickly so that the content is not
already outdated by the time it is published.

And at the very end of the preface, a note: I will address the readers
of this book as \"du\". On the one hand, this is simply my basic
attitude, and on the other, I think that my tips and hacks are then more
personal. Thus, my explanations will hopefully be better remembered and
encourage people to start implementing them immediately.

Königstein / Taunus, November 2022

Björn Radde

[]{#08_Chapter01.xhtml#page_16}**1 Introduction**

The fact is that the internet has become an integral part of our private
and business lives.^1^ It has brought about completely new patterns of
behavior and communication and will influence our behavior in the future
far more than we can currently imagine. In the last decades, the World
Wide Web has developed faster than any other medium before. Social media
have a rather recent but equally relevant history in this development
process.^2^

As much as social networks such as Facebook or Instagram have come to
dominate all our lives, for me the social media age began in December
2002 with the founding of the business network LinkedIn. And in May
2003, almost 18 years ago, the service that now belongs to Microsoft
began - making it the oldest social network still active. LinkedIn
reached the break-even point as early as April 2006. ^3^

Today\'s industry leader Facebook, on the other hand, only began in
February 2004, when founder Mark Zuckerberg still limited the service to
universities. The first major innovations, such as Facebook Chat,
followed in April 2008 and the \"Like\" button with the familiar
\"thumbs[]{#08_Chapter01.xhtml#page_17} up\" in February 2009, which has
since become a prototype for Facebook.^4^

In October 2012, Facebook became the first social media service ever to
reach the magic threshold of one billion users worldwide.^5^ In the
meantime, Facebook includes various platforms and messenger services: In
addition to the social network itself, the two messaging apps WhatsApp
and Facebook Messenger as well as the social network Instagram.

The short message service Twitter was founded in March 2006 under the
name \"twttr\" and quickly gained popularity worldwide. As early as June
2007, the platform reached a volume of 400,000 tweets sent per quarter.
In February 2016, Twitter reported declining user numbers for the first
time.^6^ In the meantime, the social network is growing again - but with
currently 337 million active users worldwide, it cannot keep up with
Facebook, Instagram & Co.^7^ Also what Elon Musk might do with Twitter
after his takeover might impact the social network.

[]{#08_Chapter01.xhtml#page_18}The youngest and currently fastest
growing network with 1 billion active members is TikTok. The Chinese
platform was founded at the end of September 2016. Users can record
short, self-made videos, which are then accompanied by music from
well-known songs or film scenes. Users usually use TikTok to make
creative and funny videos, often dancing or moving their lips in sync
with songs and scenes. The videos are limited to a length of 10 seconds.
However, TikTok, or DouYin as it is called in China, is more than just a
video platform: because users can share their videos with others, it is
also a social network.^8^ TikTok is so successful that Instagram copied
the TikTok function with Instagram Reels in summer 2020 and integrated
it into its app.

Back to LinkedIn: LinkedIn now has over 875 million users in 200
different countries and regions. The USA has the largest user base with
194 million users. This is followed by Europe with 149 million users,
India with 96 million and mainland China with 57 million users. In the
DACH region (Germany, Austria, and Switzerland), more than 19 million
people are now on the LinkedIn platform (see also figure 1).^9^

**Growth Hacking**

 

[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_26}**2 The Growth Hacking Principle**

In order to describe the growth hacking principle, the following will
briefly explain where this term comes from.

**Growth hacking history**

The term growth hacking stands for an innovative marketing approach.
Growth hackers are usually technical marketers who specialize in
optimizing landing pages, emails, and online campaigns through testing.
Sean Ellis, founder and CEO of Quarto, a website survey technology
company, coined the term in 2010 to describe a set of marketing tactics
used by start-ups whose mindset was \"growth first, budget second\".^13^

At an agile marketing meetup in San Francisco, Ellis presented a case
study on how he has applied growth hacking to his work as a
marketer.^14^ His approach was based on his extensive experience leading
growth hacking teams at Log-MeIn, DropBox, EventBrite and Lookout. Ellis
focused on a practice known as high-speed testing. This approach sets
a[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_27} target for the number of tests that
will be executed in a given time period or sprint.

Ellis reports how increasing the number of tests conducted each week led
to a significant increase in membership growth on his community website
Growthhackers.com (a great place to connect with Growth Hackers, by the
way).^15^ Once the team increased the number of tests conducted each
week, membership growth jumped. Ellis pointed to similar results
achieved by Satya Patel, former Vice President Product at Twitter. Patel
was able to massively increase membership growth when he asked the
Twitter team to move from about one test every other week to 10 tests
per week.

Because this small process change led to a big change in the company\'s
membership growth rate, Ellis challenged his team to run faster tests.
At the same time, he forced the team to break down the tests into the
most basic components possible: In the end, less complicated testing
allowed for more testing! ^16^

**What is Growth Hacking?**

To explain growth hacking, the term can be split into its two
components: Growth and Hacking.

[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_28}The word growth stands for growth,
increase or increase. This can be an increase in the reach, the number
of users, the number of customers, the turnover or, of course, an
increase in the number of followers to expand one\'s own LinkedIn
network.

Hacking means testing the limits of what is possible.^17^ A hacker does
not stick to predefined processes. Rather, he tries out everything to
achieve his goal. This requires a lot of experience, know-how,
perseverance, the right tools, and creativity.

Growth hacking is about giving an idea, a start-up, or an existing
business a substantial and, above all, sustainable boost. Sustainable
growth is much more important than short-term success.^18^

The growth hacks presented in this book are based on experience,
know-how and above all on constant trial and error. I share my
experiences with you - my growth hacks - so that you can expand your
network sustainably. I have also tried to verify these growth hacks
through research and conversations. In this respect, other successful
LinkedIn members have also made the experiences I described and achieved
similar results.

[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_29}So with the Growth Hacks, I\'m just
giving you experience and results so you don\'t have to figure it all
out yourself. However, that doesn\'t mean that you won\'t have to work
on building your network. Networking is work! However, by following my
Growth Hacks you will build up a sustainable network and probably a
little faster than without the hacks.

However, so that you don\'t have to rely on my growth hacks alone and
perhaps some of my hacks can become outdated, I would like to go into
the individual phases of growth hacking in the following. This way, you
know the methodology for developing your own growth hacks.

**2.1 Phases of growth hacking**

Growth hacking is a continuous process. The process consists of creating
ideas and hypotheses (see Figure 2).

::: fig
![](./page_29-1.jpg)
:::

*Figure 2: "The phases of growth hacking\", source: own illustration,
2022*

[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_30}These ideas are then prioritized and
tested experimentally. This is followed by the analysis of data and
findings to generate new ideas again. The cycle starts again from the
beginning.

The individual phases of the growth hacking process will be explained in
the following sections.

**2.1.1 Ideate phase**

The first phase in growth hacking is the idea generation phase. This is
where you (alone or in a team) come up with a hypothesis that you think
will lead to more growth. In the idea phase of Growth Hacking, you have
to think divergently and develop unconventional ideas for a solution.
You should first think outside the box and then gradually arrive at
realistic solutions. If you limit yourself from the beginning, you will
not come up with extraordinary solutions.

Furthermore, it is important to develop the ideas based on selected
metrics in order to measure the impact of the test ideas. So, if your
goal is to expand the LinkedIn network, the metric \"number of new
contacts\" or \"number of new followers\" would be relevant.

To generate new ideas, you can also research how other LinkedIn members
are expanding their network and try to adopt these methods. In the end,
by reading this book, you are doing just that! But you can also find
many sources of new ideas on YouTube.

[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_31}**2.1.2 Prioritization phase**

If you have generated a lot of ideas, then you have to prioritize these
ideas.^19^ One criterion is definitely time. Prioritize your ideas
according to their speed of implementation.

Another criterion is whether your idea costs a lot of money. One idea
could be that you build a bigger network as a digital marketer by flying
to Lisbon to attend the Web Summit and make contacts there. Good idea,
but you have to pay for the ticket, the flight, the accommodation, food,
taxis and so on.

Basically, I recommend implementing ideas quickly. That way you will see
successes more quickly and won\'t be demotivated. Even if your
hypothesis doesn\'t work out, it\'s a success. Please always remember
that mistakes and failures are part of growth hacking.

Another aspect why you should implement your idea quickly is the
following on LinkedIn: The first ones are usually rewarded with more
engagement, which in turn influences the reach of your network, and your
visibility on LinkedIn.

[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_32}**2.1.3 Test phase**

The testing phase in growth hacking involves executing your prioritized
ideas. In this phase, you run your defined experiments and observe how
well the idea develops.

There are two established approaches to testing in the growth hacking
process: High-speed testing and A-B testing. ^20^

In the high-speed test, several tests are to be carried out in a short
time. In an A-B test, you try out two similar ideas at the same time and
get an indication of which of your ideas works better.

By testing multiple variations, it is possible to measure which version
of a piece of content achieves the higher value, provided that
sufficient data has been collected in both variations.^21^

**2.1.4 Analysis phase**

In the literature on growth hacking, the analysis phase is also often
referred to as the first phase in the cycle. Hendrik Lennarz argues that
growth hacking is particularly based on the use of data to generate
ideas.^22^ Through the detailed analysis of user groups, their behavior
and[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_33} buying characteristics, a growth
hacker can understand how products can be improved and marketing
activities optimized.

Even though the analysis phase is usually at the beginning of the growth
hacking process in the literature, I personally see it at the end of the
cycle. Because by looking exclusively at the data at the beginning of
the idea generation and when making the hypothesis, it can happen that
it leads to a limitation of the idea. Therefore, I tend to start my
growth hacks with the question \"What if ...?\" and then think about who
can provide me with the data at the end of a test. So, I don\'t limit
myself to just my data.

If it is known where the data comes from, it is evaluated after the test
is completed. This is how the impact on the selected metrics is
determined. For your websites, you can use analysis tools such as Google
Analytics to evaluate the data from the different channels. In this way,
you can also see which traffic comes from LinkedIn to your own website.
For the analysis on LinkedIn itself, I recommend appropriate tools in
chapter 4.1 and as a Premium Member you have the possibility to view
analyses of your profile visits or your appearance in the LinkedIn
search results.

**2.2 Growth Hacking LinkedIn**

The goal of a growth hacker is to acquire users as quickly and
cost-effectively as possible.^23^ This is also reflected in the terms
used. It is about \"growth\", i.e. the growth of user numbers with the

methods. As[]{#09_Chapter02.xhtml#page_34} seen in the previous
sections, it is about trying out ideas. These ideas can be
unconventional to generate success. They may sometimes not conform to
LinkedIn guidelines, but they are not illegal. In this book you will

You have been taught the basics of growth hacking in the previous
explanations and now know theoretically how you can expand your personal
network yourself on LinkedIn: With ideas, testing and learning.

You will learn many growth hacks in this book, but you also need to
develop your own hacks to be successful. Create your own ideas, try them
out and look at the data to see if they work.

**3.2.4 Refuse to contact**

Build your network wisely. What do you want to stand for and does your
network reflect that? Also consider that you can have a maximum of
30,000 contacts in your network. Everything else are followers.
This[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_75} means you should not invite everyone
to contact you and you should not accept every contact request.

If you notice that someone is using a software and you are contacted by
a bot, do not accept this contact. The same applies if you notice that
the person has not been on your profile before. You should not accept
someone who has not dealt with you beforehand, looked at your profile
himself and then decided that you fit into his network.

You should also be consistent if someone addresses you incorrectly in
their invitation. For example, if they use the wrong first name. Or if
they refer to the fact that you both work in the financial sector, but
you work for a car manufacturer in sales. Something is wrong.

Before accepting a contact request, take a look at the LinkedIn
member\'s profile. Is the profile picture appealing? Is there a profile
picture at all? Is the work experience section filled in? Do you have
the feeling that it is a fake profile? Then leave it and do not accept
the contact request.

Finally, a very important reason for me not to accept a contact
confirmation request: Look at the posts of the person who wants to add
you. You can find them in the activity box on the LinkedIn member\'s
profile. If there are homophobic, gender-hostile or racist posts or
comments, do not accept the contact request. It is also not forbidden to
cancel contact confirmations if you notice that one of your contacts is
spreading hate speech and you discover these posts in your newsfeed. Be
consistent! It is not worth having these negative people in your
network[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_76} and poisoning your own network.
Connections with such people can also have a negative spillover effect
on you and harm you.

LinkedIn stands for internationality and diversity. LinkedIn members
help each other get ahead and affirm each other in their work. They do
not insult or discredit each other. Constructive criticism is okay, but
it is a professional network and requires a professional demeanor.

**3.3.8 Buying followers**

Now you may say that the best growth hack ever to expand your network is
to simply buy followers! Surely, with the purchase of followers, you
will be able to have a considerable number of followers on your profile.
500 LinkedIn followers cost about 40 euros on the relevant platforms, so
with an investment of less than 1,000 euros you can quickly and easily
reach 10,000 followers.

But ask yourself the question, what do you get out of it? The majority
of these bought followers are just inactive accounts taken over by bots
or fake accounts. These fake accounts will not leave quality comments
under your posts.^42^ They will also not like or otherwise interact with
any of your posts. Unless you buy likes.

Bought followers won\'t recommend your work, they won\'t make you
visible in their network, they won\'t invite you to events, they won\'t
hire you or close a deal with you, and they won\'t hire you or help you
get your next job.

[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_93}In addition to the non-existent
interaction with you, these fake accounts may share messages that you do
not identify with. These are then no longer business topics for
LinkedIn, but sometimes radical right-wing content, religious attitudes,
crude conspiracy theories or homophobic statements. Imagine one of your
business partners, colleagues or friends notices this. Then you have to
comment on it and also explain that you have bought contacts.

Another point is that LinkedIn may notice that your network has expanded
in a very short time. If the growth seems very unnatural and you receive
new followers within minutes, it can happen that your LinkedIn account
is first blocked.^43^ You will then find yourself in the so-called
LinkedIn Jail and have to wait until the facts have been clarified by
LinkedIn.^44^ While you are in LinkedIn Jail, you cannot log in to
LinkedIn.^45^

There is no shortcut to growing your network. The growth hacks described
in this book will help you grow your network a little faster. But the
work is still yours and you can\'t buy your network. Networking is work!

**Shares**

Shares on LinkedIn mean that other members re-share your original post
or that you re-share another member\'s post. This may seem like a nice
gesture at first, but it\'s not. With one exception: when an influencer
re-shares a post. Because then the author of the original post gets some
attention. However, I think that experienced LinkedIn influencers will
not re-share a post, but rather comment on an original post.

At this point, we should briefly point out the rather negative influence
of shares. On the one hand, shares do not help you. With shares, the
LinkedIn algorithm notices that the content has already been shown on
LinkedIn, and therefore this content (your share) is devalued. Secondly,
you \"steal\" engagement from the original author of the post. If you
would like the post, your contacts will also see this in their newsfeed.
The likes[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_122} of your contacts then go to
the original post. In the case of a share, only the re-shared post gets
the engagement.

Even if it is well-intentioned and statements exist that a certain reach
exists with sufficiently attractive content in the share text of the
post, from my point of view it is rather negative if you share a post
again. ^62^

[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_127}**3.4.8 Power Engagement**

Power engagement is about getting as many likes or comments on your
posts as possible, or an engagement from someone who already has a very

**LinkedIn Engagement Pods**

You can get power engagement via so-called LinkedIn engagement pods.
Engagement pods are groups of like-minded people, usually on messenger
platforms like WhatsApp or Telegram, or via browser extensions (e.g.
Lempod, Podawaa, Alcapod or LinkBoost) but also on LinkedIn itself.
These groups can quickly generate a lot of engagement. ^67^

The process is quite simple. You join these groups and whenever you post
something on LinkedIn, you send the link of your post to the group. All
group members (usually several hundred) then like your post. In return,
you have to like the posts of the other group members.^68^

::: fig1
![](./light.jpg)
:::

[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_128}***Growth Hack #93:** Join an engagement
pod at the beginning of your network building!*

This gives your post a strong boost and the LinkedIn algorithm assumes
that this post must be very interesting and then also plays it out to
other LinkedIn members. This gives your post more reach and you also
become more visible. Which in turn can lead to more contact requests.
Some pods also offer automatic networking. So, you automatically receive
new contacts.

The approach may work very well in the beginning and therefore it is
also a growth hack for me. But in the long run, LinkedIn pods have the
following disadvantages, and you really shouldn\'t use them for
long.^69^

[▪ ]{.bull-num}The same people always respond to your posts. The
LinkedIn algorithm will therefore assume that this group is very
interested in your content and will therefore play out your posts more
to the people in the engagement group in the future. Thus, you will
hardly be able to expand your network.

[▪ ]{.bull-num}Another disadvantage is that you also have to like the
content of the others or tools can automatically like or even comment
for you. Otherwise, you will be excluded from the group. However, if you
do not agree with the content of the other members, if it does not
correspond to your values or if the contributions are
not[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_129} congruent with your own brand, then
you will quickly get into trouble. Imagine that the Engagement Pod
automatically likes a political statement that you absolutely do not
support. All your contacts could theoretically see this like and put you
in a political corner. This can even harm you considerably if you use
radical right-wing or racist slogans.

[▪ ]{.bull-num}Another disadvantage is that other LinkedIn members can
see who has liked your posts. If only 2nd and 3rd degree contacts
interact with your posts, this also looks very strange. Someone who
knows LinkedIn well will immediately see that you are using an
engagement pod.

[▪ ]{.bull-num}The biggest downside, however, is that LinkedIn\'s
community guidelines state that you should not try to artificially
increase the number of views, shared posts, likes and comments you make.
^70^ LinkedIn engagement pods, however, are there to do just that. In
this respect, LinkedIn may suspend your account. Therefore, you should
be very careful when using engagement pods and not overdo it.

My recommendation is to start your own but small LinkedIn engagement
pod. Open a WhatsApp group or simply an email list with contacts you are
familiar with and know well. E.g. friends or family members.
But[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_130} your circle of colleagues is also
ideal for this. ^71^ Especially if you work in a big company or group,
you should send the link to your post to your colleagues and ask them to
interact with your post (like, share or comment). Imagine getting 20 or
30 quality interactions on your post in no time. This is a very powerful
boost for your content.

::: fig1
![](./light.jpg)
:::

***Growth Hack #94:** Start your own LinkedIn engagement pod!*

However, you should not ask this group to interact with your post and
also to read or linger on the post every time you post.^72^ Use the
power of the group only very selectively. For example, if you are going
to be a speaker or have published a new book and want the information to
be widely distributed. But also if you have written your own LinkedIn

However, if you ask your private LinkedIn engagement pod for support

**3.4.9 Engagement-purchase**

As mentioned in the previous section, buying a power like can help you
gain more reach and visibility. Personally, however, I would not go for
a purchase. You should be so good with your content and address your
target group so precisely that an influencer also recognizes your work
and gives you a corresponding engagement in the form of a like or a

LinkedIn members can see who has liked your posts. If only second and

contacts and inconsistent profile content can like your post. This looks

[▪ ]{.bull-num}Another disadvantage is that bought engagement doesn\'t

[▪ ]{.bull-num}If you are self-employed and artificially boost your

even be classified as misleading advertising. Your competitors could
then warn you according to § 3 and § 5 para. 1 UWG and you could be
sentenced to a fine.^75^ You should be aware of this disadvantage of the
purchase.

I like to repeat myself: Networking is work. In this respect, you cannot
avoid creating good content to increase your visibility. You also have
to[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_134} actively expand your network. Buying
followers and likes will not help you.

[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_144}**3.7 Social Selling Index**

Before I go into the Social Selling Index, I would like to briefly
explain what social selling actually is: Social selling refers to the
research, establishment and maintenance of business contacts or customer
relationships in social media. The aim is to establish close customer
contacts and create long-term added value.^79^

In a traditional sales process, there is a person selling something (the
seller) and someone being sold to (the buyer). Cold calling is often
used to generate leads and then convert them. The disadvantage of this
approach is a very low success rate, as salespeople usually know very
little about the prospects. In today\'s social and digital world, there
are more effective and easier ways to connect with prospects. This is
where social selling comes in and offers significant benefits for both
parties:

[▪ ]{.bull-num}Sales staff can better identify potential customers and
connect with them online.

[▪ ]{.bull-num}Prospects, in turn, use their business networks to find
help with their buying decisions and build trust during the
decision-making process.

Through social selling, salespeople are able to identify the actual need
that needs to be addressed on the sales side. Perhaps the prospect
wants[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_145} to purchase a new product or
service because his current product does not meet certain requirements.
It may also be that he has no need at all but is looking for solutions
that will make his life better, easier or more pleasant.^80^ For
sellers, it is important to use these sales channels to gather
information in preparation for the first conversation. But it is also
about discovering social commonalities. Were the seller and the prospect
perhaps at the same university? Or do they have a common contact? All
this information makes selling easier.^81^

Through social selling, salespeople can increase their visibility among
potential customers and build trust. On the one hand, they can
strengthen the relationships they have built with customers and, on the
other hand, follow their activities in order to place the right offer at
the right moment.

Conversely, this also means that social selling on LinkedIn does not
mean combining a contact request with a pitch. If you want to sell
something on LinkedIn, then formulate your invitations without
obligation. Generate a certain amount of trust with added value over
time and at some point the day will come when you can present your
product or your solution.

[]{#10_Chapter03.xhtml#page_146}Good social selling also allows
potential customers to actively approach a salesperson because they are
aware of the activities on social media and have developed trust.

To see how good sellers are at social selling, LinkedIn provides the
Social Selling Index (SSI). The good thing is that this index is not

The SSI tells you how effective you are at establishing your own
professional brand. It tells you whether you are networking with

topics yourself or engage with the posts of others. In this respect, the
SSI is a good indicator for you to improve your personal LinkedIn
presence. Since the index is updated every day, you can regularly check

**4.1 LinkedIn analysis tools**

LinkedIn offers you basic analytics, but to learn properly from your
data, good analytics tools help you. I have listed the most popular ones
below.

**4.1.1 Shield**

If you like LinkedIn, you will love Shield. Because it should be the
best analytics tool for LinkedIn.^85^ Shield offers you a comprehensive
data analysis of all your previously published posts on LinkedIn. The
most important KPIs are recorded and visually displayed on a dashboard.
In addition to the well-known metrics, many other properties are
displayed to see at a glance what kind of content works best.^86^

For example, you know when your posts are working best and you need to
post again. So-called visual benchmarks show you whether your last post
worked better or worse than your previous posts.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_157}You can test Shield for ten days for
free. After that, the cheapest version starts at currently six dollars,
followed by the Creator version for 12 dollars and the Influencer
version for 19 dollars.

**4.1.2 InLytics**

InLytics promises that you can use the tool to improve your content in
order to increase your influence. Similar to Shield, InLytics offers the
possibility to collect the data of your LinkedIn posts and to analyze
them with additional data.^87^

Currently InLytics is available in three price categories (Free, Solo
and Business). For testing purposes, I recommend you install the free
version. However, you can only collect and analyze data after the
installation. For historical data you will have to pay a fee.

**4.1.3 Klipfolio**

With Klipfolio you can build comprehensive dashboards and visualize data
from a variety of sources. In addition to Twitter, Facebook, Instagram
and YouTube, you can also use LinkedIn. Unlike Inlytics or Shield,
however, Klipfolio\'s data is related to company pages, so the tool
doesn\'t help you build your own personal brand or grow your private
network. However, for the sake of completeness, I wanted to mention this
tool here, because you can build a LinkedIn dashboard with
Klipfolio[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_158} relatively easily. Maybe the
developers of Klipfolio will extend the functionalities to private
profiles in the future.^88^

No matter which analytics platform you use. The important thing is that
you use your data. Since data plays an essential role in growth hacking,
it stands to reason that using analytics tools is a growth hack.

**4.2 LinkedIn Automation Tools**

LinkedIn automation tools help you to automate certain processes. For
example, there are tools that visit the profiles of other LinkedIn
members or tools that send automated invitations. The following tools
are the best-known and most interesting on the subject of LinkedIn
automation.^89^

**4.2.1 Linked Helper**

Probably the best-known tool when it comes to LinkedIn automation is
Linked Helper. It was once just a Chrome extension. However,
the[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_159} development team has released a
standalone app and is no longer reliant on a risky browser
integration.^90^

As with its predecessor, numerous actions can be completely automated:
Contact requests, messages, visiting profiles and much more. ^91^

The built-in CRM provides the ability to better manage and overview
contacts and campaigns on LinkedIn. It also has better security compared
to other Chrome browser extensions, smart reply detection (to avoid
embarrassing messages) and Zapier connectivity.

**4.2.2 Dux Soup**

The Google Chrome extension Dux-Soup performs actions in your browser
while it is open. Dux-Soup allows you to visit the profiles of other
LinkedIn members free of charge. This way you can possibly provoke a
contact. In the paid version, Dux-Soup also automatically contacts other
LinkedIn members. In addition to the usual automations, Dux-Soup also
offers the confirmation of skills and automatic and personalized message
workflows that are stopped as soon as the corresponding profile has
contacted you. ^92^

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_160}I personally have tried Dux-Soup a few
times and the provocation of a contact by a profile visit works quite
well. However, the profile visits happen so quickly one after the other
that you have to be careful that the LinkedIn bots don\'t notice the
machine visits and your LinkedIn account is temporarily suspended.

**4.2.3 Alfred**

Alfred is probably the most comprehensive LinkedIn automation tool.
Here, too, you can automate various actions. The difference, however, is
that Alfred is a separate application for your computer and therefore
more secure than a browser extension.^93^

Alfred also offers its own LinkedIn CRM to manage and categorize your
LinkedIn contacts. Of course, this always includes the matching of
LinkedIn data.

**4.3 LinkedIn Productivity Tools**

To make the daily work on LinkedIn more productive, it is possible to
use numerous helpers. This is not just about automation on LinkedIn, but
a combination of analysis and lead generation. The goal here is to
increase one\'s success. Be it closing sales, promoting a product or
expanding one\'s own network. In the following, I briefly introduce
the[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_161} two most relevant LinkedIn
productivity tools. For me, the most important are the LinkedIn Sales
Navigator and Zapier.

**4.3.1 LinkedIn Sales Navigator**

If you want to use LinkedIn to increase your sales, the LinkedIn Sales
Navigator is a very powerful tool. It is offered directly by LinkedIn
and is designed to connect customers and sellers in a unique way.^94^

The LinkedIn Sales Navigator has many functions. The following are the
most important:

[▪ ]{.bull-num}Using an advanced algorithm to find the best potential
customers for your offer.

[▪ ]{.bull-num}A relationship-building tool that will help you
continually move closer to your goal.

As you receive recommendations for relevant people on LinkedIn, you can
use the many features of Sales Navigator to reach potential customers
and better market your products.

The LinkedIn Sales Navigator is so comprehensive that I could write a
book about it, and several books have already been published about the
[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_162}Sales Navigator.^95^ Therefore, I do not
want to go into further detail about the LinkedIn Sales Navigator at
this point. However, one thing should be mentioned: With or without
Sales Navigator, a good LinkedIn profile and a good network are
important!

**4.3.2 Zapier**

Zapier is a tool that offers LinkedIn automations. The big difference,
however, is that these tasks are handled via LinkedIn\'s official API
and therefore there is no risk of being blocked.^96^

The possibilities for automation are therefore somewhat smaller here.
You can only automatically publish a post on your profile. However, it
is important to mention here that the content can come from any source.
Zapier now has interfaces to over 3,000 applications.

Besides a connection to LinkedIn, you can also integrate Twitter,
Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, but also Hubspot, Mailchimp or Slack.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_163}**4.4 LinkedIn profile data tools**

A well-maintained profile on LinkedIn says a lot. There are some tools
that prepare this data very well and there are some providers that can
collect further data from the vastness of the internet and make it
available to you. I would like to introduce you to a few of them here.

**4.4.1 Crystal Knows**

Crystal Knows analyzes any LinkedIn profile and generates a
comprehensive personality profile of the person. In addition, it
provides concrete recommendations for establishing contact and the right
approach to the contact or potential customer.^97^ Crystal even goes so
far as to provide you with concrete formulations for establishing
contact.

However, the tool is not only used to analyze a specific person. You can
also use the tool to sample your target group and analyze what content
they might like. You can then post this content.

I strongly recommend that you try out this browser extension. First test
the tool on your own profile and check how well Crystal Knows identifies
your psychological characteristics. I found it very exciting the first
time and was at the same time surprised, almost shocked, how well the
tool screened me.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_164}**4.4.2 Chartloop**

Chartloop automatically creates an organization chart of any company.
The process is very simple. After selecting the company (via URL), you
just select the department. For example, Marketing, Controlling, or
Procurement. And then you give Chartloop some time. After a couple of
hours, you will receive an email with a link to your generated chart
including name, position and (if found) email address and phone
number.^98^ Chartloop is an excellent tool for anyone on LinkedIn who
works in sales and is looking for contacts in the company. But job
seekers can also benefit from it if they want to know who they should
write to in the company to have a better chance of applying.

 

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_165}**4.5 LinkedIn publishing tools**

The more comprehensive your content strategy is on LinkedIn, the sooner

**4.5.1 Buffer**

Buffer is probably one of the most well-known providers when it comes to
the automatic publication of social media posts. I\'ve known Buffer for
years and use the tool especially for my Twitter posts. But you can also
use Buffer for LinkedIn.^100^

However, I think Buffer has one small drawback when used with LinkedIn.
You can upload alternative photos for your post on Buffer if you don\'t
like the image of the original article dragged via the Open Graph.
However, LinkedIn ignores this and the original image of the article
always appears.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_166}**4.5.2 Hootsuite**

Similar to Buffer, Hootsuite allows you to schedule and automatically
publish your LinkedIn posts. However, you have the advantage that you
can also tag other LinkedIn pages and thus make your posts even more
interactive.^101^

In addition, Hootsuite allows you to create your own so-called streams
and control content from different social media networks in your
dashboard. Personally, I find the calendar with all planned and used
posts very good. Here you can easily reuse your content for different
channels.

**4.5.3 Planable**

When it comes to the planned publication of social media posts, Planable
is certainly also a very good tool. Here, too, you can publish your

The graphical design of Planable\'s user interface deserves a special
mention. You can move your posts in your content calendar with a simple
drag-and-drop operation. In addition to LinkedIn, you can also plan all
your other social media channels such as Instagram, TikTok, Twitter,
Facebook or YouTube.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_167}You can try Planable first before you
want to use it permanently and then have to pay. Try it out!

**4.5.4 Social Pilot**

With Social Pilot you can plan your LinkedIn posts based on data very
well and publish them automatically. This means you appear very
professional on LinkedIn and other social media channels. Companies and

particularly practical to obtain content via external RSS feeds. You
could theoretically automatically publish the content of your blog on
LinkedIn. ^103^

If you\'re regularly looking for new ideas for your content, be sure to
check out Social Pilot\'s content curation section. It\'s also great
that you can manage all your social media communication via Social
Pilot.

A shortcoming of Social Pilot is the monthly price. Although you can try
the tool for free, it will cost you 50 dollars a month after that. All
in all, the tool is more for very professional social marketers or for
small and medium-sized companies as well as larger companies.

But if you\'re regularly looking for new ideas for your content, you
should definitely check out the content curation section of Social
Pilot.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_168}**4.5.5 Drumup**

Drumup is a very good tool for finding and curating content. Drumup also
allows you to schedule your posts in advance, making your social media
management a little easier.^104^

Unfortunately, Drumup is only available in English and the curated
content is also only provided in English. However, if you are part of an
international network on LinkedIn, Drumup is an excellent tool to shine
with good content. I find it particularly practical that I also receive

So far, I\'ve been using Drumup for one keyword completely free of
charge and I can\'t currently see when it will become chargeable. So,
it\'s worth trying it out quickly. However, each additional keyword

Since publishing tools are not forbidden by LinkedIn, I definitely

**4.6 LinkedIn content tools**

Often the question arises, where do you get good content to create good
posts? Drumup is already a good tool for suggesting content. In the
following, I would like to give you a few more tools that can help you
generate ideas for good content.

**4.6.1 Answer The Public**

From my perspective, a real goldmine for good content ideas is the
website answerthepuplic.com.^105^ On the website, you can enter a
keyword and get a list of questions that other internet users have asked
about this keyword.

I often enter the keyword \"digital marketing\" here and then get a list
full of questions on the topic. For example, \"Which digital marketing
courses are the best?\". This is the start of a good content idea. I
feel inspired to write an article about good digital marketing courses.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_170}The website alsoasked.com works in a
similar way. Here, too, you can enter a term and then get a mind map
with questions on the topic. You can then download the mind map as a png
file. ^106^

**4.6.2 Quora**

Quora works similarly to Answer The Public.^107^ First you have to
select a field of interest (e.g. technology or business). Within these
fields you can then choose sub-topics and get a newsfeed full of
questions from other users. Here you can answer the questions
immediately or you can use these questions for your content creation as
described in the example of Answer The Public.

**4.6.3 Twitter**

It may sound banal, but Twitter is an excellent source of good content
and idea generation.^108^ Search for your keyword or hashtag in the
Twitter search and you will get a multitude of tweets matching your
topic. Take a look at the tweets and the linked articles and create your
own new content for LinkedIn.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_171}**4.6.4 AuthoredIn**

AuthoredIn is a browser extension to preview and format your posts and
keep your drafts in one place without leaving LinkedIn.^109^ So, you can
see how your post will look in the feed. In addition, you can stylize
your post to catch attention with bold, italic, or other font styles.
You also are able to add emojis. And you can save your posts for later.

The browser extension is completely free of charge in beta and makes
your live as content creator a little bit easier!

**4.6.5 Remove.bg**

To conclude the chapter, here is a tool that does not help you generate
ideas but can support you in creating content: Remove.bg.^110^ BG is
known to be the top-level domain for Bulgaria, but here it stands for
Background. Remove.bg is therefore a tool to remove the background of
photos.

You can upload any photo of yourself and the website removes the
background perfectly. I use the tool a lot because I can add a
contextual background to my posts.

[]{#11_Chapter04.xhtml#page_172}In chapter 3.4.1 I told you about the
possibility of putting yourself in front of logos to push your post.
With Remove.bg you can insert any logo in the background.

The tool is free if you accept low-resolution photos as the result. As a
rule, the resolution is sufficient for your posts. If you need
high-resolution images, you have to pay per image.

[]{#12_Chapter05.xhtml#page_175}**5 Conclusion and outlook**

As I hope you have discovered in this book, there are many ways to grow
your network on LinkedIn and build your personal brand. I hope you
haven\'t heard of many of the growth hacks and that they have helped
you. Let me know and get in touch with me on LinkedIn.

In the final chapter of this book, it should be noted that not all hacks
work for everyone. Therefore, it is important that you understand the
growth hacking principle. You have to try many small things to be
successful as a whole. You have to test every day and learn every day.
Even hacks that might work today are no longer possible tomorrow.
Platforms evolve, after all.

The fact that platforms evolve brings me to one of the last tips in this
book. Even though I am an absolute fan of LinkedIn and spend a lot of my
free time on LinkedIn, I recommend a multi-channel strategy when
building your personal brand. Imagine LinkedIn changes so much that you
don\'t like it anymore. Or LinkedIn loses its relevance again. Finally,
there have been many social networks that no longer exist. StudiVZ or
Second Life are just two examples. Google+ was also completely
discontinued by Google. Likewise, Foursquare no longer has the
significance it once had. In addition, new social media platforms such
as TikTok, Polywork, BeReal, or Mastodon are emerging that may quickly
replace existing platforms.

Imagine if you had focused solely on Google+ for your self-branding.
Then you would have had 8 years to build your personal brand, but
all[]{#12_Chapter05.xhtml#page_176} your brand building would be invalid
because Google closed the social network in April 2019. ^111^

In my opinion, a multi-channel strategy makes sense. This means that you
should be active on several social media channels. Besides LinkedIn, I
personally recommend Twitter and YouTube in particular. But you should
also take a look at new platforms like TikTok, whereby you must not lose
the focus on your professional personal brand.

[]{#12_Chapter05.xhtml#page_178}**Outlook**

I would also like to give an outlook on the topic of self-marketing and
raising one\'s own profile at this point and thus conclude the book.

Ever since I saw the documentary MIIO in 2011, I have been fascinated by
the topic of self-marketing and optimizing personal brands in the
digital world. The film \"My Identity Is Open\" (MIIO) shows us the
world as it might look in a few years. It tells the story of Damian, who
lives in a society where MIIO technology is widely used. MIIO is a
mobile device with which Damian can scan the people around him. Via
facial recognition but also via GPS localization, he gains access to an
extensive pool of data on his counterpart. Name, age, place of
residence, preferences and so on. MIIO also provides information about a
person\'s state of health or criminal history.^113^ MIIO also provides a
percentage indication of whether two people would suit each other as
partners.

I am convinced that optimizing oneself will become important in the
future in the increasingly digitalized world. When looking for a new
job, a good presence on the internet is already relevant today. LinkedIn
is an excellent platform for this and will play an even more important
role in the future.^114^ However, it is also conceivable that a good
reputation and a professional appearance could become relevant for other
issues. I think[]{#12_Chapter05.xhtml#page_179} that in the future there
will be so-called social media profilers who will work full-time on
optimizing people on the internet or on social networks. This will not
only optimize the job search, but also credit allocation, health
insurance, housing allocation, choice of partner and so on.

If your life is online, then unfortunately you also have to seriously
consider what should happen to your social media profiles in the event
of your own demise. Should your LinkedIn profile continue to exist as if
nothing had happened? Do you want someone you trust to delete it? Or
would you like a notice to appear on your profile that you are no longer
alive? Talk about it in confidence with your partner or a good friend.
Remember to leave your access data to the network.

Finally, I would also like to talk about the risks of self-presentation.
By this I don\'t mean that you will also receive criticism for your
activities in social media. Here my clear advice is: never mind! There
are other dangers, such as cyber-bullying, cyber-stalking and, above
all, identity theft.^115^ I don\'t want to go into detail about these
issues here, but I recommend that you take them seriously.

All in all, though, I think social media is an unprecedented opportunity
for each of us. Your smartphone is your TV studio and the social media
channels are the broadcasters. You have to move from being a
content[]{#12_Chapter05.xhtml#page_180} consumer to a content producer
to make yourself visible. Today\'s media world is open to all of us and
we should use it!
